- theta=0.2: CONDENSES (top firm 52%, 97% of firm-time at floor): sublinear self-term cannot hold a big firm whose LINEAR interaction field crushes its neighbours.
- With consistent interactions (alpha S^theta) the model is the original GLV in u = S^theta (up to the normalisation term): a power transform, so bimodality survives and beta_S = theta beta_u. Dead end.
No-go reading: in one-variable d lnS = Phi - F(S), beta comes from the restoring rate F'(S) S, and the scale where it switches on is where the attractor sits; every firm has two attractors (plateau ~ fitness, or extinction), so small firms are only transits. Matches the earlier trade-off beta = 1.12 x density slope.

Granular-volatility map (Monte Carlo, 2026-10-11): if a firm's volatility is sigma*sqrt(Herfindahl of its n partners' sizes), with partner sizes Pareto(zp) and n ∝ x^gamma over 1-300 partners, then beta = c(zp)*gamma and ratios(zp) are: zp=1.0 -> c .20, ratios 1.71 2.17 2.46; zp=1.3 -> .29, 1.78 2.30 2.61; zp=1.8 -> .39, 1.90 2.60 3.06; zp=2.2 -> .44, 1.96 2.82 3.47. MSB ratios (2.0 2.6 2.9) match zp ≈ 1.5-1.8, which with beta=0.2 means gamma ≈ 0.5-0.6. The asymptotic formula min(q(1-1/zp), zp-1) is far off at these n. Caveat: this needs partners that persist; refreshing partners within a year averages the Herfindahl and pushes the ratios toward q.
Round 2 scope_share (s0=2e-3): the giant is NOT a surviving founder (no founder is alive after burn 150). The cause is a near-critical tail, zeta - 1 ~ s0. Score caveat: entrants born at 2e-3, just above the listing threshold LIST=1e-3, with high volatility leave the listing within a year, so the smallest spells are biased toward calm firms. That gives a spurious negative local beta at the small end (vol 0.33 at 1e-3 rising to 1.2 at the top).
Partner model theory (2026-10-11). scripts/screen/theory_partner.py is a mean-field reduction, d ln x = nu dt + sqrt(2 D H) dW. It matches the full model at N=1000, gamma=1 (beta .079 vs .078, aggregate vol .133 vs .134) and is about 100x cheaper. Map (N=4000): s0=0.2, gamma 1/1.5/2 gives beta .16/.24/.33 with Hill 1.4-1.6 and top share <0.09. s0=0.05 gives Hill ~1 but top share ~0.2. The ratios always come out above q (≈2.1, 3.3, 4.6) and the sigma/sigmabar tail b is 6-7.7. Structural reason: an averaging field has H ≤ 1, so volatility is bounded; there is no dispersion at n=1 (below s0), and hence no power-law tail of sigma/sigmabar. A summing field (GLV) has unbounded volatility but blows up next to Zipf giants.
Hybrid and exit tests (reduced model, 4 seeds, 2026-10-11):
- Breadth heterogeneity k_i ~ Pareto(3.3), with v² ∝ H k, moves b from 7.5 to 5.5-5.9, but the ratios stay near q (2.05-2.13, 3.0-3.3, 3.9-4.5). Configs that looked data-like on one seed have a seed sd of 2-4.
- Exit by decline (absorbing at 1e-3 m) instead of hazard kills turnover (life = whole window), gives Hill 2-6, and ratios exactly q. Dead end.
- Theorem behind it: if sigma_i = sigmabar(S) * eta_i with eta independent of S (separable), then zeta_q = q*beta exactly. Ratios < q need a size-independent volatility component whose weight declines slowly with size; in the repo GLV under spells this came from life-cycle climb/fall transients.
Key realisation (2026-10-11): the partner/scope_share model is a granular (sub-unit) model with partners as the sub-units, the class MSB 2024 argue against. Gaussian shocks averaged over partners make big firms' growth Gaussianise (data: no Gaussianisation). The size-dependent H spread breaks sigma/sigmabar universality, and the breadth k_i patch only fixes b. The ratios come out ≈ q. Interactions add only endogenous sub-unit sizes and weak comovement (~n/N). Way out, following MSB's lead: correlations that grow with size (Herskovic et al. 2020). In model terms, propagation g_i = own shock + rho sum_j h_ij g_j, so interactions generate the correlations (the user's original wish).

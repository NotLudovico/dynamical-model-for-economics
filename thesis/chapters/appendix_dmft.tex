\chapter{Dynamical mean-field theory of the relative model}
\label{app:dmft}
\providecommand{\Dz}{\mathcal{D}z}
\providecommand{\E}{\mathbb{E}}

This appendix derives a heterogeneous dynamical mean-field theory (HDMFT) for the scale-invariant
model of the growing-economy chapter.  The derivation starts from the absolute sizes $x_i(t)$ and
keeps their time-dependent mean $m(t)$ explicit.  Only after deriving the coupled equations for
$m(t)$ and the relative sizes $S_i(t)=x_i(t)/m(t)$ do we apply the network HDMFT of
\citet{aguirrelopez} and its structural-cutoff extension by \citet{patil2026}.  The result is a
degree-resolved stochastic process coupled through the full degree-to-degree connection kernel.
The regular fully connected theory, including $\sigma_c=\sqrt{2}$, is recovered as a one-class
limit rather than assumed for the power-law graph.

We retain $\lambda$ during the exact change of variables and in the time-dependent effective
process.  We set $\lambda=0$ only when deriving the clipped-Gaussian fixed point and its linear
stability.  The numerical model uses $\lambda=10^{-3}$, whose effect on the phase boundary can
therefore be tested separately from degree heterogeneity.

\section{The model and its reduction to a replicator}
\label{sec:dmft-model}

Write the relative GLV in a competition-positive convention,
\begin{equation}
    \dot x_i
    =x_i\left[1-\frac{x_i}{m(t)}-\frac{(\beta x)_i}{m(t)}\right]
      +\lambda\bigl[m(t)-x_i\bigr],
    \qquad
    m(t)=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N x_j(t),
    \label{eq:dmft-glv}
\end{equation}
with couplings on a graph of mean degree $C$,
\begin{equation}
    \beta_{ij}=A_{ij}\left(\frac{\mu_{\rm c}}{C}
                  +\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt C}z_{ij}\right),
    \qquad z_{ij}\sim\mathcal N(0,1),\qquad
    \overline{z_{ij}z_{ji}}=\gamma .
    \label{eq:dmft-couplings}
\end{equation}
Here $\mu_{\rm c}>0$ means competition.  It is related to the sign convention of the main text by
$\mu_{\rm c}=-\mu$, so the operating point $\mu=-1.76$ is $\mu_{\rm c}=1.76$ in this appendix.
Equivalently, $\beta=-W$.  This notation matches the convention of \citet{patil2026} and avoids
carrying two compensating minus signs through the derivation.

Define the time-dependent relative size
\begin{equation}
    S_i(t):=\frac{x_i(t)}{m(t)},
    \qquad \frac1N\sum_i S_i(t)=1.
    \label{eq:dmft-relative-size}
\end{equation}
Because $m$ is a function of time, differentiating $x_i=mS_i$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \dot x_i(t)=\dot m(t)S_i(t)+m(t)\dot S_i(t).
    \label{eq:dmft-product-rule}
\end{equation}
Substituting $x_i=mS_i$ into \eqref{eq:dmft-glv} removes $m(t)$ from every ratio,
\begin{equation}
    \dot m\,S_i+m\dot S_i
    =mS_i\bigl[1-S_i-(\beta S)_i\bigr]
     +\lambda m(1-S_i).
    \label{eq:dmft-before-average}
\end{equation}
Now average this identity over firms.  Since $\langle S\rangle=1$, $\langle\dot S\rangle=0$,
and the immigration term has zero mean, one obtains
\begin{align}
    \dot m(t)
      &=m(t)\left\langle S_i(t)
          \bigl[1-S_i(t)-(\beta S(t))_i\bigr]\right\rangle_i,
          \label{eq:dmft-mdot-step}\\
    \frac{\dot m(t)}{m(t)}
      &=:g(t)=\langle S(t)F(t)\rangle,
    \qquad F_i(t):=1-S_i(t)-(\beta S(t))_i.
    \label{eq:dmft-g}
\end{align}
Thus $g(t)$ is not introduced as an independent constant: it is the instantaneous logarithmic
growth rate of the time-dependent mean size.  Inserting $\dot m/m=g(t)$ back into
\eqref{eq:dmft-before-average} gives the exact composition dynamics
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{\;
    \dot S_i(t)=S_i(t)\bigl[F_i(t)-g(t)\bigr]
                  +\lambda\bigl[1-S_i(t)\bigr].
    \;}
    \label{eq:dmft-replicator}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:dmft-g} and \eqref{eq:dmft-replicator} are equivalent to the original
$x$ dynamics.  The scale is recovered at any time from
\begin{equation}
    m(t)=m(0)\exp\left(\int_0^t g(s)\,ds\right),
    \qquad x_i(t)=m(t)S_i(t).
    \label{eq:dmft-reconstruct-scale}
\end{equation}
The $-g(t)$ term is therefore exactly the quotient-rule correction associated with the moving
denominator $m(t)$.  It also preserves the constraint: averaging
\eqref{eq:dmft-replicator} gives $\langle\dot S\rangle=\langle SF\rangle-g\langle S\rangle
+\lambda(1-\langle S\rangle)=0$.  In the thermodynamic limit $g(t)$ is a deterministic,
self-averaging common field, but it generally remains a function of time in the fluctuating phase.

\section{The graph kernel and its structural cutoff}
\label{sec:dmft-kernel}

Let $p$ label a degree class, let $\rho_p$ be the fraction of nodes in that class, and let
$P_{pr}$ be the probability that a node in class $p$ is connected to a node in class $r$.
Write
\begin{equation}
    c:=\frac{C}{N},\qquad v_p:=\frac{k_p}{N}.
\end{equation}
The kernel must reproduce the observed class degrees and the global mean degree,
\begin{equation}
    v_p=\sum_r\rho_rP_{pr},\qquad
    c=\sum_p\rho_pv_p=\sum_{p,r}\rho_p\rho_rP_{pr}.
    \label{eq:dmft-kernel-degree}
\end{equation}
The uncorrelated Chung--Lu approximation $P_{pr}=v_pv_r/c$ fails whenever
$v_pv_r>c$, since it would assign a connection probability above one.  This is precisely the
structural-cutoff problem of the hubs in a broad power-law graph.  Following \citet{patil2026},
introduce positive hidden degree fractions $h_p$ and use the soft-configuration kernel
\begin{equation}
    P_{pr}^{\rm SCM}=\frac{h_ph_r}{h_ph_r+c}.
    \label{eq:dmft-scm-kernel}
\end{equation}
The $h_p$ are fixed by substituting \eqref{eq:dmft-scm-kernel} into
\eqref{eq:dmft-kernel-degree}.  For $h_ph_r\ll c$ the denominator reduces to $c$ and the
ordinary bilinear kernel is recovered.  For hubs, $P_{pr}^{\rm SCM}$ saturates below one and
retains the disassortative correlations induced by the simplicity constraint.  The theory below
accepts any symmetric kernel satisfying \eqref{eq:dmft-kernel-degree}; the SCM form is the
ensemble prediction used for the production graph.

\section{From the network field to the time-dependent HDMFT}
\label{sec:dmft-cavity}

For a node $i$ in class $p$, split the interaction field in \eqref{eq:dmft-g} into its mean and
random parts,
\begin{align}
    (\beta S)_i(t)
      &=\frac{\mu_{\rm c}}{C}\sum_jA_{ij}S_j(t)
        +\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt C}\sum_jA_{ij}z_{ij}S_j(t)
        \nonumber\\
      &=:H_i^{(\mu)}(t)+H_i^{(z)}(t).
    \label{eq:dmft-field-split}
\end{align}
There are $N\rho_r$ potential neighbours in class $r$, each present with probability $P_{pr}$.
Consequently, averaging first over nodes in class $r$ and then over classes gives
\begin{align}
    \E[H_i^{(\mu)}(t)\mid p]
      &=\frac{\mu_{\rm c}}{C}
        \sum_rN\rho_rP_{pr}\E[S_r(t)]\nonumber\\
      &=\mu_{\rm c}B_p(t),
    \label{eq:dmft-mean-field-step}\\
    B_p(t)&:=\frac1c\sum_r\rho_rP_{pr}\E[S_r(t)].
    \label{eq:dmft-B}
\end{align}
The same calculation at two times gives the covariance of the zero-mean random part,
\begin{align}
    \E[H_i^{(z)}(t)H_i^{(z)}(s)\mid p]
      &=\frac{\sigma^2}{C}\sum_rN\rho_rP_{pr}
          \E[S_r(t)S_r(s)]\nonumber\\
      &=\sigma^2q_p(t,s),
    \label{eq:dmft-noise-cov-step}\\
    q_p(t,s)&:=\frac1c\sum_r\rho_rP_{pr}\E[S_r(t)S_r(s)].
    \label{eq:dmft-q}
\end{align}
In the high-connectivity limit the central limit theorem therefore replaces $H_i^{(z)}$ by a
Gaussian process $-\sigma\eta_p(t)$ with zero mean and
$\E[\eta_p(t)\eta_p(s)]=q_p(t,s)$.  The sign is immaterial because the Gaussian law is
symmetric.  The production ensemble has $\gamma=0$, so there is no retarded Onsager term.
For $\gamma\ne0$, the degree-resolved response kernel of \citet{aguirrelopez} must be added.

Substitution into the exact relative dynamics \eqref{eq:dmft-replicator} yields one effective
process for every degree class,
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{\;
    \dot S_p(t)=S_p(t)\left[1-S_p(t)-\mu_{\rm c}B_p(t)
                       +\sigma\eta_p(t)-g(t)\right]
                    +\lambda\left[1-S_p(t)\right].
    \;}
    \label{eq:dmft-heterogeneous-process}
\end{equation}
This equation is still fully time dependent.  In particular, $g(t)=\dot m(t)/m(t)$ has not
been replaced by a stationary constant.  The stochastic processes close through
\eqref{eq:dmft-B}--\eqref{eq:dmft-q} together with
\begin{align}
    1&=\sum_p\rho_p\E[S_p(t)],
    \label{eq:dmft-normalisation}\\
    g(t)&=\sum_p\rho_p\E\!\left[
       S_p(t)\bigl(1-S_p(t)-\mu_{\rm c}B_p(t)+\sigma\eta_p(t)\bigr)
       \right].
    \label{eq:dmft-growth-closure}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:dmft-growth-closure} is not an additional approximation.  Averaging
\eqref{eq:dmft-heterogeneous-process}, using \eqref{eq:dmft-normalisation}, and imposing
$d\langle S\rangle/dt=0$ reproduces it.  Once the coupled effective processes are solved,
$m(t)$ follows from \eqref{eq:dmft-reconstruct-scale}.  The composition theory and the scale
equation are therefore coupled through $g(t)$ but the growing scale never enters the interaction
statistics separately.

\section{Stationary fixed point without immigration}
\label{sec:dmft-fixed-point}

Now assume that the relative composition reaches a unique fixed point.  Then
$g(t)\to g^*$, $B_p(t)\to B_p^*$ and $q_p(t,s)\to q_p^*$ at long times.  Set
\begin{equation}
    a:=1-g^*.
    \label{eq:dmft-a}
\end{equation}
For a surviving firm, $S_p^*>0$, the bracket in
\eqref{eq:dmft-heterogeneous-process} must vanish.  For an extinct firm the non-negative
solution is zero.  Hence
\begin{equation}
    S_p^*(z)=\left[a-\mu_{\rm c}B_p^*
                   +\sigma\sqrt{q_p^*}\,z\right]_+,
    \qquad z\sim\mathcal N(0,1),\qquad [u]_+:=\max(u,0).
    \label{eq:dmft-clipped-law}
\end{equation}
Define the class threshold
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_p:=\frac{a-\mu_{\rm c}B_p^*}{\sigma\sqrt{q_p^*}}.
    \label{eq:dmft-delta-p}
\end{equation}
The firm survives when $z>-\Delta_p$.  With
\begin{equation}
    w_n(\Delta):=\int_{-\Delta}^{\infty}\Dz\,(\Delta+z)^n,
    \qquad \Dz:=\frac{e^{-z^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,dz,
\end{equation}
the three required Gaussian integrals are
\begin{align}
    w_0(\Delta)&=\Phi(\Delta),\nonumber\\
    w_1(\Delta)&=\Delta\Phi(\Delta)+\phi_{\rm G}(\Delta),\nonumber\\
    w_2(\Delta)&=(1+\Delta^2)\Phi(\Delta)
                    +\Delta\phi_{\rm G}(\Delta),
    \label{eq:dmft-gaussian-moments}
\end{align}
where $\Phi$ and $\phi_{\rm G}$ are the standard-normal cumulative distribution and density.
It follows step by step that
\begin{align}
    \varphi_p&:=\Pr(S_p^*>0)=w_0(\Delta_p),
    \label{eq:dmft-class-survival}\\
    M_{1,p}&:=\E[S_p^*]
       =\sigma\sqrt{q_p^*}\,w_1(\Delta_p),
    \label{eq:dmft-class-first}\\
    M_{2,p}&:=\E[(S_p^*)^2]
       =\sigma^2q_p^*\,w_2(\Delta_p).
    \label{eq:dmft-class-second}
\end{align}
Substitution of these moments into \eqref{eq:dmft-B}, \eqref{eq:dmft-q}, and the scale
normalisation gives the closed fixed-point system
\begin{align}
    B_p^*&=\frac1c\sum_r\rho_rP_{pr}M_{1,r},
    \label{eq:dmft-fixed-B}\\
    q_p^*&=\frac1c\sum_r\rho_rP_{pr}M_{2,r},
    \label{eq:dmft-fixed-q}\\
    1&=\sum_p\rho_pM_{1,p},
    \label{eq:dmft-fixed-normalisation}\\
    g^*&=1-a.
    \label{eq:dmft-fixed-growth}
\end{align}
For $n$ degree classes, these are $2n+1$ equations for the $2n+1$ unknowns
$\{B_p^*,q_p^*\}_{p=1}^n$ and $a$.  The aggregate scale is then
$m(t)\sim m(0)e^{g^*t}$, but the stationary law \eqref{eq:dmft-clipped-law} concerns the
relative sizes only.

\subsection{Finite immigration}

For the simulated model $\lambda>0$, no class is exactly extinct and the clipped law must be
replaced before testing stability.  Define the Gaussian local field
\begin{equation}
    h_p(z):=a-\mu_{\rm c}B_p^*+\sigma\sqrt{q_p^*}\,z.
\end{equation}
The stationary version of \eqref{eq:dmft-heterogeneous-process} is
\begin{equation}
    0=S_p^*(h_p-S_p^*)+\lambda(1-S_p^*),
\end{equation}
or, after collecting powers of $S_p^*$,
\begin{equation}
    (S_p^*)^2-(h_p-\lambda)S_p^*-\lambda=0.
\end{equation}
The non-negative root is
\begin{equation}
    S_p^*(z;\lambda)
      =\frac{h_p(z)-\lambda
        +\sqrt{[h_p(z)-\lambda]^2+4\lambda}}{2}.
    \label{eq:dmft-immigration-root}
\end{equation}
It is strictly positive for every finite $h_p$ and converges to $[h_p]_+$ as
$\lambda\downarrow0$.  The class moments in
\eqref{eq:dmft-class-first}--\eqref{eq:dmft-class-second} are consequently replaced by
direct Gaussian averages of \eqref{eq:dmft-immigration-root}; the field equations
\eqref{eq:dmft-fixed-B}--\eqref{eq:dmft-fixed-normalisation} are otherwise unchanged.

The zero-frequency response to a perturbation of $h_p$ follows by implicit differentiation.
From the quadratic equation,
\begin{equation}
    \left[2S_p^*-(h_p-\lambda)\right]\frac{\partial S_p^*}{\partial h_p}=S_p^*.
\end{equation}
Using $(h_p-\lambda)=S_p^*-\lambda/S_p^*$ from the same quadratic gives
\begin{equation}
    \chi_p(z):=\frac{\partial S_p^*}{\partial h_p}
       =\frac{(S_p^*)^2}{(S_p^*)^2+\lambda}.
    \label{eq:dmft-immigration-response}
\end{equation}
This response, rather than a binary survival indicator, is the factor that enters the stability
calculation.

\section{Linear stability with the scale constraint}
\label{sec:dmft-stability}

Define the class response weight
\begin{equation}
    R_p(\lambda):=\E_z[\chi_p(z)^2].
    \label{eq:dmft-response-weight}
\end{equation}
The structural-cutoff HDMFT then gives the random, zero-frequency stability operator
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{pr}(\lambda)
       =\frac{\sigma^2}{c}\,R_p(\lambda)P_{pr}\rho_r.
    \label{eq:dmft-stability-matrix}
\end{equation}
To see its origin, let $Y_p$ be the variance of a small persistent perturbation in class $p$.
The perturbation arriving from class $r$ has variance
$\sigma^2P_{pr}\rho_rY_r/c$, and the squared local response averages to $R_p$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
    Y_p=\sum_r\mathcal L_{pr}Y_r.
    \label{eq:dmft-stability-eigenproblem}
\end{equation}
The unique fixed point loses bulk stability when the Perron root reaches one,
\begin{equation}
    \rho(\mathcal L)=1.
    \label{eq:dmft-stability-condition}
\end{equation}
When $\lambda\downarrow0$, $\chi_p\to1$ on the surviving branch and $\chi_p\to0$ on the
extinct branch, so $R_p(0)=\varphi_p$.  The stability operator then reduces exactly to the
survival-weighted expression of \citet{patil2026}.

At $\lambda=0$ the relative model also contains the common perturbation $-\delta g$.  It is
useful to show explicitly what this changes.  At a fixed point, for perturbations tangent to
$\langle S\rangle=1$,
\begin{align}
    \delta g
      &=\frac1N\sum_i\left[\delta S_iF_i^*
          +S_i^*\delta F_i\right]\nonumber\\
      &=-\frac1N\sum_iS_i^*\left[\delta S_i+(\beta\delta S)_i\right],
    \label{eq:dmft-delta-g}
\end{align}
where the first term in the first line is $g^*\langle\delta S\rangle=0$.  Consequently,
with $D=\operatorname{diag}(S^*)$ and $L=I+\beta$, the finite-system Jacobian on the surviving
set is
\begin{equation}
    J=-D\left(I-\mathbf 1\frac{(S^*)^{\!T}}{N}\right)L.
    \label{eq:dmft-projected-jacobian}
\end{equation}
The extra factor is a rank-one projection that removes the forbidden scale direction.  It does
not change the extensive random bulk whose variance instability is
\eqref{eq:dmft-stability-condition}, but it can move an isolated coherent eigenvalue at finite
$N$.  We therefore use \eqref{eq:dmft-stability-condition} as the HDMFT prediction for the
onset of persistent fluctuations and use \eqref{eq:dmft-projected-jacobian} as a separate
finite-size diagnostic in numerical validation.
For $\lambda>0$, the local diagonal response is the susceptibility
\eqref{eq:dmft-immigration-response} and the derivative of $g$ acquires additional
$\lambda/S_i^*$ weights.  These contributions remain diagonal plus rank one; their random-bulk
effect is already contained in $R_p(\lambda)$ in \eqref{eq:dmft-stability-matrix}.

\section{Homogeneous limit}
\label{sec:dmft-homogeneous-limit}

For one regular degree class, $\rho_1=1$ and $P_{11}=c$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:dmft-fixed-B}--\eqref{eq:dmft-fixed-q} reduce to $B^*=M_1=1$ and
$q^*=M_2$.  The mean competition $\mu_{\rm c}B^*=\mu_{\rm c}$ is then a uniform shift and is
absorbed into $a$; it cannot affect the relative-size distribution.  The stability condition is
\begin{equation}
    \sigma^2\varphi=1.
\end{equation}
At the transition the homogeneous clipped-Gaussian equations give $\Delta=0$ and
$\varphi=\Phi(0)=1/2$, hence
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_c=\sqrt{2}.
\end{equation}
On a heterogeneous graph $B_p^*$ and $q_p^*$ vary with degree, so mean competition is no longer
a uniform shift.  Both $\varphi_p$ and the Perron root in
\eqref{eq:dmft-stability-condition} depend on $\mu_{\rm c}$.  The power-law theory therefore
predicts a curve $\sigma_c(\mu_{\rm c})$, not the horizontal fully connected line.

\section{Numerical solution}
\label{sec:dmft-numerical-solution}

The implementation bins the realised degree distribution into classes, solves
\eqref{eq:dmft-kernel-degree} for the hidden degrees of the SCM kernel, and then solves
\eqref{eq:dmft-fixed-B}--\eqref{eq:dmft-fixed-normalisation} for
$\{B_p^*,q_p^*,a\}$.  For $\lambda>0$, the Gaussian averages of
\eqref{eq:dmft-immigration-root} and \eqref{eq:dmft-response-weight} are evaluated by
Gauss--Hermite quadrature.  At every parameter pair the solver forms
\eqref{eq:dmft-stability-matrix}; a scalar root search in $\sigma$ locates the point where its
largest eigenvalue is one.  The one-class limit is checked numerically against the closed regular
DMFT and recovers its growth rate, survival fraction, second moment, and
$\sigma_c=\sqrt{2}$ to numerical precision.  On a representative production graph at
$N=4000$, the SCM theory moves the critical disorder from $\sigma_c=\sqrt2$ to a
$\mu_{\rm c}$-dependent curve.  At $\mu_{\rm c}=1.7$ it gives $\sigma_c=1.409$ for
$\lambda=0$ and $1.411$ for $\lambda=10^{-3}$.  The stored finite-time churn classifier switches
near $1.57$--$1.60$, so immigration explains very little of the residual displacement.  The next
validation must therefore isolate finite observation time and the operational churn cutoff before
changing the HDMFT equations.

\chapter{Numerical methods and measurement protocol}
\label{app:numerics}

\section{Integration}

The relative model is integrated in physical time, with the composition kept in bounded
variables and the aggregate scale carried in its logarithm. This avoids numerical overflow despite
exponential aggregate growth. Internally the solver evolves the shares
$w_i = S_i/N$, which live on the probability simplex and are renormalized onto it at
each output time; we integrate \eqref{eq:replicator} in this form
together with $\mathrm{d}\ln m/\mathrm{d}t = g_{\mathrm{eff}}$ with an explicit
Runge--Kutta solver throughout. At the system sizes reached in the text ($N$ up to
$3.2\times10^4$) the implicit solvers we tried switched to a dense Jacobian and became
prohibitively slow, whereas the explicit method integrates one run to $t = 250$ in about
ten seconds at $N = 10^4$.

\section{Volatility estimator}

The per-firm growth-rate volatility is measured with the mean-absolute-deviation
estimator
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_i = \sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{2}}\,\big\langle\, \big|\,g_i - \langle g_i\rangle\,\big| \,\big\rangle ,
\end{equation}
rescaled by the Gaussian-consistent factor $\sqrt{\pi/2}$ and used in place of the
standard deviation for robustness against the fat tails of the growth rates.

\section{Size--volatility fit}

The immigration floor makes the smallest firms quiet, producing a plateau at the bottom
of the size range. We therefore fit $\sigma(\bar S)$ only along the monotone decline above the
plateau and report the coefficient of determination $R^2$, rather than impose one slope on a
curve that is not a power law throughout. The exponent quoted in the text, $\beta = 0.45 \pm 0.06$, is
the median and economy-to-economy standard deviation of this decline-branch slope over
twenty independent disorder realizations at $N = 16000$; the spread is set by the disorder
realization rather than the fit, whose pooled-decline $R^2$ is $0.99$. All exponents,
including the higher moments $\zeta_q$, are fitted per economy and averaged
(Section~\ref{sec:growing-estimator}); pooled fits are quoted only for comparison.

\section{Robustness of the measurement protocol}
\label{sec:robustness-protocol}

The protocol of Chapter~\ref{chap:firmgrowth} contains two arbitrary numerical choices:
growth rates are read over the fixed horizon $\Delta t = 0.5$, and the trajectories are
integrated at relative tolerance $10^{-4}$. This section varies both on the graph of the
main text (power law $2.5$, $C = N/40$) at $N = 8000$, ten economies, and re-measures the
three headline statistics: the size--volatility exponent $\beta$, the quenched moment
ratios $\zeta_q/\zeta_1$, and the excess kurtosis of the growth-rate distribution.

For the horizon, each economy is integrated once and stored at fine output resolution,
and every $\Delta t$ from $0.25$ to $4$ is measured on the same trajectory, so any
difference between the columns of Figure~\ref{fig:robustness-protocol} is an estimator
effect and not a resampling of the dynamics. The moment ratios do not move: over a factor
of sixteen in $\Delta t$ they stay at $1.86,\,2.55,\,3.1$ with every shift inside one
standard error. The exponent is flat within the economy-to-economy spread up to
$\Delta t = 1$ ($\beta = 0.40 \pm 0.07$ at $\Delta t = 0.25$ against $0.41 \pm 0.07$ at
the production value) and then climbs steadily, $0.49 \pm 0.05$ at $\Delta t = 2$ to
$0.58 \pm 0.06$ at $\Delta t = 4$: beyond the correlation time of the chaotic state the
increments stop sampling the local diffusion and start sampling the firm's stationary
spread, and the median volatility saturates accordingly. The correlation time is measured
on the same trajectories: the firm-averaged autocorrelation of $\ln S$ is $0.82$ at lag
$1$, $0.54$ at lag $2$ and $0.18$ at lag $4$, a half-life of $2.3 \pm 0.3$ time units, so
the flat range $\Delta t \le 1$ sits below half the correlation time and the drift sets
in where $C(\Delta t)$ decays. The horizon is therefore part of the definition of $\beta$, as it
is in the data, where the exponent is reported at a yearly horizon. The ratios
$\zeta_q/\zeta_1$ show no comparable dependence. Identifying the production horizon
$\Delta t = 0.5$ with one year places the firm memory time at four to six years; every horizon
below that time returns the same exponent. The excess kurtosis falls from $9.2$ to $4.2$
over the same range as successive increments aggregate toward the Gaussian; the tent
stays above the Laplace reference at every lag.

For the tolerance, the first three economies are re-integrated at relative tolerance
$10^{-6}$ (absolute $10^{-9}$) and measured at the production horizon. Individual
trajectories in a chaotic state cannot coincide across tolerances; the statistics do.
The per-economy exponents move from $0.59,\,0.47,\,0.41$ to $0.58,\,0.48,\,0.45$, a
largest shift of $0.04$ against the $\pm 0.07$ spread between economies, and the moment
ratios and kurtosis shift within their standard errors.

\chapter{Between-firm growth correlations}
\label{app:correlations}

\citet{moran2024} argue that granular models fail because they omit a correlation between firms'
growth rates that strengthens with firm size: ``the correlation of a firm's growth rate with that
of the whole economy and the correlation of the growth rate of firms of similar sizes clearly grow
with their size''. The model studied here reproduces the size-conditioned statistics through the
degree heterogeneity of its interaction graph. The natural question is whether it does so through
the same size-growing correlations or through a different channel.

We measure two correlations from the per-firm growth time series $g_i(t) = \Delta \ln S_i$ in the
fluctuating state, on the thesis graph (power-law $2.5$, mean degree $C = N/40$, $N = 16000$, ten
economies). The first is the co-movement of a firm with the aggregate,
$\rho_i = \mathrm{corr}_t\!\big(g_i(t),\,\bar g(t)\big)$, where $\bar g(t)$ is the
cross-sectional mean growth: it measures how strongly a firm tracks the common motion of the
economy. The second is the mean pairwise correlation
$\mathrm{corr}_t\!\big(g_i(t),\,g_j(t)\big)$ among firms that fall in the same bin, the direct
analogue of \citet{moran2024}'s ``correlation of firms of similar sizes''. Each is binned both by
firm size and by degree.

Both measures are small and, within the resolved range, nearly flat
(Figure~\ref{fig:correlations}). Co-movement with the aggregate is about $\rho \approx 0.09$,
with no visible dependence on size or degree. The same-bin pairwise correlation is roughly an
order of magnitude smaller, $\approx 0.01$, with at most a weak rise to $\approx 0.018$ among the
largest firms. The correlations identified by \citet{moran2024} are therefore present but modest,
and they do not increase appreciably with either size or degree.

The size-conditioned anomalies therefore arise here \emph{without} the size-growing correlations
conjectured by \citet{moran2024}. Instead, the dilute normalization makes the amplitude of a
firm's idiosyncratic noise increase with its connectivity
(Section~\ref{sec:growing-graph}). That is a single-firm mechanism operating against a modest,
largely flat background of between-firm correlations. The model is thus an alternative to
\citet{moran2024}'s hypothesis, rather than a realization of it.

Two qualifications matter. The pairwise measure averages over same-bin pairs that are almost all
unconnected on the sparse graph, so it may understate correlations between direct trading partners.
Moreover, the survival mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:growing-degree-size} removes the extreme
hubs from the fluctuating state: the live-firm degree axis reaches only $k \approx 470$, although
the graph tail extends beyond $k \approx 3000$. A direct test on firm-level data with a known
interaction network would remove both restrictions.

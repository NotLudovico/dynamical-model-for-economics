\chapter{Firm-size distribution}
\label{app:size-distribution}

The model produces a persistent hierarchy of firm sizes, but that alone does not imply the broad
Zipf-like distribution observed for firms and wealth. Figure~\ref{fig:size-distribution} measures
the stationary distribution of relative sizes $S_i=Nw_i$ at the thesis operating point. For each
of ten independent $N=10000$ economies, we form the distribution over the late window
$t\in[180,240]$ and then average the complementary distribution functions with equal economy
weight. The economies are not pooled into one supergraph.

The left panel retains all observations. Its lower plateau is shaped by the immigration term. The
reporting cut $S=0.1$ removes this floor-dominated region only for the upper-tail panel; it is not
a graph cutoff. On log--log axes the active-firm tail bends sharply rather than following an
approximately straight Pareto segment. Estimating the complementary-tail exponent separately in
each economy from its upper $2\%$ of active observations gives $\hat\mu=4.40$, with a $95\%$ $t$
interval $[4.02,4.78]$. A Zipf tail has complementary exponent near one. At this operating point,
the model therefore has a much thinner, effectively truncated upper tail.

This narrows the thesis claim. The main results concern size-conditioned growth fluctuations and
their origin in interaction-network heterogeneity, not a quantitative account of the
cross-sectional firm-size distribution. Matching both would require an extension of the present
relative GLV specification.
